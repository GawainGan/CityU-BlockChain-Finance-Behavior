## 2. What Do I Want to Do Next?

1. **Verify terminology accuracy.** Further cross-check all terminology used in the six-layer matrix and diagnosis files against actual protocol mechanisms, eliminating any residual semantic confusion.
2. **Verify whether protocol scope affects data requirements.** The current design uses Aave V3 as the primary protocol with Compound/MakerDAO analyzed separately. I need to confirm whether this scope setting affects the completeness and availability of data needed for the research, and assess whether the protocol coverage range needs adjustment.
3. **Literature search and reading.** Around the revised research questions (position management behavior and liquidation propensity), continue searching for and reading the latest literature on DeFi lending borrower behavior, liquidation prediction, and risk signals.
4. **Understand Oracle mechanisms and their impact on on-chain asset values.** I have noticed that the value of on-chain assets is not determined entirely by intra-chain transactions — it also depends on an "invisible" external component: the oracle. The oracle's role is to bring external world information (such as market prices of assets) into on-chain protocols. In Aave V3, for example, it uses [Chainlink](../../04_阶段成果_Milestone_Deliverables/2026-08-11_构念效度审查与数据验证_Construct_Validity_Review_and_Data_Verification/2026-08-11_六层矩阵_Paper1_Six_Layer_Matrix/03_技术文档_Technical_Docs/04_Chainlink预言机_Chainlink_Oracle.md) as the price oracle; Chainlink aggregates prices from multiple external data sources and submits them on-chain. This means the "price" the protocol sees is not generated within the chain — it is a mapping of the external world. If the oracle experiences update latency, price bias (e.g., Chainlink's `minAnswer`/`maxAnswer` mechanism can limit price reporting in extreme scenarios), or OEV (Oracle Extractable Value)-related mechanisms that affect liquidation timing and profitability, it directly affects the protocol's core computations: HF changes, liquidation triggers, collateral valuation, borrowing capacity, etc. — which in turn affect borrowers' repayment, staking, and liquidation behavior. In other words, the oracle is the channel through which "the external world influences the on-chain world." What I need to figure out is: in my research, should I treat the oracle as an external factor that needs to be modeled and analyzed separately, or can I treat on-chain transactions (including oracle inputs) as a unified whole without disentangling "external world impact on the on-chain world"? This is a question about research boundary definition. I will first search for and read literature on oracle mechanisms and their impact on DeFi protocols before deciding how to handle it.

## 3. What Issues or Difficulties Am I Having?

- **Prospect Theory positioning and identification challenge.**

  **Background**: Prospect Theory (Kahneman & Tversky) is a behavioral economics theory about how people make decisions under risk — people do not evaluate outcomes in absolute terms, but relative to a "reference point." Above the reference point (gains), people tend to be risk-averse; below it (losses), people tend to be risk-seeking. Moreover, losses feel more painful than equivalent gains feel pleasurable (loss aversion).

  **How it was used in Report v1**: I used Health Factor (HF) = 1.0 as the borrower's psychological reference point. The theoretical expectation was that borrowers would anchor to HF=1.0, with behavioral patterns shifting as HF approaches 1.0. PT was positioned as the "confirmed theory anchor" of the research. Technical details of HF are in the [Health Factor concept file](../../04_阶段成果_Milestone_Deliverables/2026-08-11_构念效度审查与数据验证_Construct_Validity_Review_and_Data_Verification/2026-08-11_六层矩阵_Paper1_Six_Layer_Matrix/02_逐概念六层矩阵_Per_Concept_Matrices/03_Health_Factor_健康因子_Health_Factor.md) and the [Aave V3 technical document](../../04_阶段成果_Milestone_Deliverables/2026-08-11_构念效度审查与数据验证_Construct_Validity_Review_and_Data_Verification/2026-08-11_六层矩阵_Paper1_Six_Layer_Matrix/03_技术文档_Technical_Docs/01_Aave_V3协议_Aave_V3.md).

  **The problem**: HF=1.0 is not just a psychological reference point — it is simultaneously the protocol's mechanical liquidation threshold. When HF drops below 1.0, any liquidator can trigger liquidation. Therefore, the behavioral change observed around HF=1.0 has two possible explanations:

  - Explanation A (psychological mechanism): Borrowers perceive HF approaching 1.0 and exhibit risk-averse or risk-seeking behavioral reactions (Prospect Theory).
  - Explanation B (mechanical mechanism): Protocol rules produce a structural change at HF=1.0 — borrowers' behavioral changes are mechanically triggered by the protocol, not psychologically driven.

  These two explanations produce identical observable patterns in the data (behavioral discontinuity at HF=1.0). I cannot distinguish which mechanism is at work from the data alone. Therefore, I downgraded PT from "confirmed theory anchor" to "compelling competing explanation" — not because PT itself is wrong, but because I cannot prove that the behavioral change is driven by the psychological mechanism rather than the mechanical mechanism. The detailed diagnosis and revision process is in the [Prospect Theory diagnosis file](../../04_阶段成果_Milestone_Deliverables/2026-08-11_构念效度审查与数据验证_Construct_Validity_Review_and_Data_Verification/2026-08-11_诊断改进映射_Paper1_Diagnosis_and_Fix/02_逐问题诊断与改进_Per_Problem_Diagnosis/06_Prospect_Theory定位_PT_Positioning.md).

  **My confusion**: I am not sure whether this "downgrade" is the right treatment, and I do not know whether a better identification strategy exists to separate these two explanations.

- **Reconstruction cost for HF and Debt.**

  **Background**: The data feasibility verification confirmed that all input data is available on Dune (see [Feasibility Assessment](../../04_阶段成果_Milestone_Deliverables/2026-08-11_构念效度审查与数据验证_Construct_Validity_Review_and_Data_Verification/2026-08-11_数据可行性验证_Paper1_Data_Feasibility/02_数据可行性评估_Feasibility_Assessment.md)), but "available" does not mean "ready to use." HF and Debt are not stored as historical snapshots in Aave V3 — the protocol only records events (Supply, Borrow, Repay, Liquidation, price updates, etc.). The researcher must reconstruct HF and Debt values for each position at each point in time from these events. Technical details of HF are in the [Health Factor concept file](../../04_阶段成果_Milestone_Deliverables/2026-08-11_构念效度审查与数据验证_Construct_Validity_Review_and_Data_Verification/2026-08-11_六层矩阵_Paper1_Six_Layer_Matrix/02_逐概念六层矩阵_Per_Concept_Matrices/03_Health_Factor_健康因子_Health_Factor.md); debt reconstruction requirements are in the [Aave V3 technical document §5 Interest Rate Mechanism](../../04_阶段成果_Milestone_Deliverables/2026-08-11_构念效度审查与数据验证_Construct_Validity_Review_and_Data_Verification/2026-08-11_六层矩阵_Paper1_Six_Layer_Matrix/03_技术文档_Technical_Docs/01_Aave_V3协议_Aave_V3.md).

  **The difficulty**: This means processing the full event history of every position, maintaining running state snapshots, handling interest accrual and price updates, and dealing with edge cases (e.g., position transfers, EMode switches, Isolation Mode). The computational cost depends on the number of positions and events, which could be very large. Additionally, edge-case error handling (e.g., LT value jumps during EMode switches) can easily introduce reconstruction errors. This is feasible, but the cost and error risk need careful evaluation.

- **Protocol scope uncertainty.**

  **Background**: "Protocol scope" refers to which DeFi lending protocols the study covers. Report v1 originally planned to pool data from three protocols — Aave, Compound, and MakerDAO — into a single panel dataset for analysis.

  **The problem**: These three protocols have fundamentally different architectures — different design purposes (Aave is a general-purpose liquidity pool, Compound III is a single-base-asset market, MakerDAO is a stablecoin issuance protocol), leading to differences in risk metrics, position structures, liquidation mechanisms, interest rate mechanisms, and oracle mechanisms. More critically, the "liquidation risk" metrics they use are not on the same scale: Aave V3 uses Health Factor (HF < 1 = liquidatable), Compound III uses Account Shortfall (shortfall > 0 = liquidatable), and MakerDAO uses Collateralization Ratio. This means the "liquidation risk" concept is not comparable across protocols — pooling them is not just "potentially misleading" but **conceptually incomparable** — analogous to pooling bank loan data and pawnshop loan data together without distinguishing them.

  **Further findings**: I have compiled a technical comparison document of the three protocols' design purposes, mechanism differences, and impact on the research ([Cross-Protocol Comparison](../../04_阶段成果_Milestone_Deliverables/2026-08-11_构念效度审查与数据验证_Construct_Validity_Review_and_Data_Verification/2026-08-11_六层矩阵_Paper1_Six_Layer_Matrix/03_技术文档_Technical_Docs/07_协议间异同对比_Cross_Protocol_Comparison.md)). Technical details for each protocol: [Aave V3](../../04_阶段成果_Milestone_Deliverables/2026-08-11_构念效度审查与数据验证_Construct_Validity_Review_and_Data_Verification/2026-08-11_六层矩阵_Paper1_Six_Layer_Matrix/03_技术文档_Technical_Docs/01_Aave_V3协议_Aave_V3.md), [Compound III](../../04_阶段成果_Milestone_Deliverables/2026-08-11_构念效度审查与数据验证_Construct_Validity_Review_and_Data_Verification/2026-08-11_六层矩阵_Paper1_Six_Layer_Matrix/03_技术文档_Technical_Docs/02_Compound_III协议_Compound_III.md), [MakerDAO](../../04_阶段成果_Milestone_Deliverables/2026-08-11_构念效度审查与数据验证_Construct_Validity_Review_and_Data_Verification/2026-08-11_六层矩阵_Paper1_Six_Layer_Matrix/03_技术文档_Technical_Docs/03_MakerDAO与Sky_MakerDAO_Sky.md). The comparison revealed several structural differences that affect research design:

  - **Different liquidation mechanisms**: Aave and Compound use instant liquidation (single transaction), while MakerDAO uses an auction-based process (which may last from minutes to hours). The temporal definition of a "liquidation event" is not uniform across protocols.
  - **The PT identification problem is Aave-specific**: The dual nature of HF=1.0 (psychological reference point + mechanical threshold) exists only in Aave. Compound's Shortfall=0 and MakerDAO's Ratio=LR are purely mechanical boundaries with no reason to believe borrowers would psychologically anchor to them. Therefore, the PT identification problem cannot be resolved through cross-protocol comparison.
  - **The meaning of "external validity checks" needs redefining**: Observing "similar behavioral patterns on Compound/MakerDAO" should not be equated with "results are externally valid." The focus should be on "robustness of behavioral patterns" (whether pre-liquidation active adjustment still exists under different mechanisms) rather than "comparability of behavioral parameters."

  **The revision**: Changed to Aave V3 as the sole primary protocol for in-depth analysis. Compound/MakerDAO to serve as subsequent external validity checks (not to be invested in now), focused on "robustness of behavioral patterns" rather than "parameter consistency."

  **Remaining uncertainty**: I have not yet confirmed whether Compound III and MakerDAO have the same level of decoded table availability and event granularity on Dune as Aave V3. However, based on the comparison analysis, the current recommendation is to proceed with Aave V3 as a single-protocol study for the core analysis, and assess whether external validity checks are warranted after the core results are obtained.

  **Connection among the three difficulties above**: These three difficulties are not isolated — they form an implicit logical chain. The oracle's price input directly affects the accuracy of HF reconstruction (if oracle prices systematically deviate from market prices, the reconstructed HF values themselves may be distorted). The accuracy of HF values, in turn, directly affects PT identification (if the observation that "behavioral change occurs around HF=1.0" is contaminated by oracle price distortion, distinguishing between the psychological and mechanical mechanisms becomes even more difficult). In other words: Oracle → HF reconstruction → PT identification — the three problems are sequentially dependent. This is also why I have prioritized Oracle mechanisms as a research direction in my next steps.
